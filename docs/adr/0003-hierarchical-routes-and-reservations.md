# Hierarchical routes and space-time reservations

Long routes (flat estimate over 48 blocks) run on a region layer over the 16³ navigation tiles; short routes, escapes from a build designation, nearest-safe-pose searches, traffic detours and engineering stay on flat A*. A region record holds its poses, its in-region edges, its exits and one reverse distance field per exit. An exit is a run of crossings into one neighbouring region, joined while neighbouring crossings step to each other both ways on both sides and capped at 8, so a single middle representative keeps reachability exact. Exits are directed: a drop has no exit back up. Records are built on demand, sliced, and shared by every query waiting on the same tile. The layer reads geometry only, plus one static rule: a pose whose support block falls is not a region pose, because the live policy always rejects it. Water, hazards, build reservations and units stay live checks in refinement and execution.

Every region a route reads is built exactly, so an exhausted search proves the goal unreachable: policy can only remove routes. A cut by node limit, overflow, confinement, exclusion or memory stays `limited`, and a tile that changed between reads ends the query `stale`; the start and goal tiles are pinned when the query is made. Route searches are A* over exits, and group fields are one reverse search from a move order's destinations toward the boxes of its units' starts. Both weight the heuristic by 1.25, which bounds a route at 1.25× the abstract optimum; without the weight, rough terrain ties A* across whole rectangles of regions. Measured against exact flat A* on fuzzed hills, routed pairs cost 1.016× on average (p95 1.07, max 1.20) and reachability agreed on every pair. Units refine the route two segments ahead by descending the exit fields under the live policy, fall back to a region-confined search, exclude a failed exit and replan, and replan from where they stand when a segment still to walk changed.

The region store has a byte cap and evicts in insertion order. Records index their slots by column, and empty tiles share one frozen record. Build work is charged against the navigation budget at `BUILD_WEIGHT` steps per operation. The store is derived and not hashed, but operation counts depend on it, so replays are exact only from tick 0; a future snapshot or restore must rebuild the store deterministically, or hash it. Queries in flight, shared builds and units' routes are hashed.

Walking units reserve (cell, tick) pairs over a rolling 32-tick window, and units standing still hold their cells with no end. The first owner of a (cell, tick) keeps it. When a projection collides, the unit with the lower priority repairs it with a space-time search in a small box: it rejoins its route further on, or ends in a pocket that nobody needs past the window. Priority is the order's priority plus aging for ticks without progress, and ties rotate with the tick instead of favouring an id. Occupancy remains the source of truth for collision; the table plans.

Conflicts are resolved best right of way first, at most 8 per tick: higher aged priority, then a rotation that changes every 64 ticks, then the lower id. A unit takes the right of way from an idle owner or one already yielding to it and, once stuck (no new best distance for 20 ticks), from one of lower priority, provided the owner finds a refuge off its route; when only the stuck unit has a refuge, the roles reverse. Otherwise an idle owner, or an idle unit next to either of them, walks off the route within 16 blocks. A working unit is never displaced, and a unit that yielded keeps yielding for 200 ticks. Repairs prefer the right-hand lane. After 160 stuck ticks the earlier detour, sidestep and traffic-block rules take over. Known limit: dense opposing flows still jam (30 against 30 in a 128-long two-wide corridor); 20 against 20 passes, slowly.
